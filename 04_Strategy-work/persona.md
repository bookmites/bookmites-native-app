

**Great Books Shelf**
Syllabus / prestige — books people put on lists and assign in school. Shakespeare, Austen, Tolstoy, Walden, Marcus Aurelius, Britannica Great Books, Guardian/BBC/Modern Library titles. Some of that is dense (philosophy, Conrad). A lot of it isn’t (Pride and Prejudice is not pulp, and it isn’t a slog either).


**Old Popular Shelf**
Pastime fiction that aged into the public domain — what people actually bought to be entertained, 1880–1930 or so. Mystery, adventure, early SF, comic novels, series. Holmes, Christie, Verne, Wells, Wodehouse, Tarzan, Hardy Boys, Scarlet Pimpernel. Pulp is the loudest corner of this pile, not the whole pile. Wodehouse is light, not pulp. Trollope is Victorian social novel, not Burroughs.

### **Technical Sophistication.** 
Gretta started collecting book files in college because it was cheaper than buying everything off Kindle. Classmates helped her discover Gutenburg as a place to get things, and she is a very-low-level user of Calibre. 

Gretta understands what "not-a-cloud-service" means because she moves her own files around. She has a Google Drive, but the concept of backing an app up to it would still be new and novel to her. 

Gretta does not enjoy "tech." She is happy to move files around, and doesn't want to be locked into SaaS companies, but she does not have a background in tech and does not wish to learn about technology. 

*Mobile first, mobile mostly*
Gretta has a computer that she uses for work, but it mostly gathers dust. 90% of her digital interactions are on mobile.

### **Book Acquisition**
Gretta has a library full of existing epubs that she's downloaded in the past because she wanted to read them (she's read some of them). She's kinda-haphazardly tracked them in Calibre, but the interface isn't very good, and she feels that it isn't really meant for someone like her (a passive, non-tech-savvy collector). She wishes there was an easier way to just browser her collection. 

She is also interested in finding new books, but doesn't want a corporation constantly shoving things in her ace whenever she opens an app. She wants to opt-in to browsing, not have it forced on her. 


### **Propensity to pay**
Gretta is anti-SaaS. She doesn't have that much money and doesn't believe in getting sucked int subscriptions. She is happy to pay money for things when she can (she likes buying indie comics, buying used books from the book store, supporting creators on paytreon), but feels strange paying money for classics. She also feels strange paying "rent" on her own notes, highlights, and annotations.
